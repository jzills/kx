A watch is no longer only something to look at: the rows it draws are numbered, so you can act on what you see changing without listing it again.

- **Numbered watches:** On a terminal, `kx get pods -w` numbers each row as it appears, and `kx describe 3` in another terminal reaches the row drawn as 3.
- **Numbers that stay put:** A row keeps its index while it changes or moves under `--sort`, and a deleted row's index is never given to another resource.
- **Two terminals at once:** When you list something else, the watch stops numbering and says so in its footer: `another listing is now current — showing a live view; press Ctrl-C to stop`.
- **Sorted events for agents:** The `events` tool on `kx mcp` takes a `sort`, over the same columns as `kx events --sort`.

**Breaking:** If you relied on `kx get -w` leaving your current listing alone, list again after the watch, or point `KX_STATE` at its own file for the watch: a watch on a terminal now saves its rows as your current listing once the first one arrives.
